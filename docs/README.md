# OctoAcme Project Management Process Overview

This repository captures the project management approach used by OctoAcme to guide cross-functional work from idea to release. The process follows a clear lifecycle: initiation, planning, execution, release, and continuous improvement. During initiation, teams validate the business need, define success metrics, identify stakeholders, and document the high-level vision in a lightweight project one-pager. Once the work is approved, the team moves into planning to break the effort into a prioritized backlog, estimate effort, and map milestones, dependencies, and release timing.

OctoAcme relies on defined roles and clear ownership to keep delivery coordinated. Product leaders set the direction, prioritize value, and define outcomes; project managers maintain the plan, risks, schedule, and communication rhythm; developers implement, test, and improve the work; QA validates quality and acceptance criteria; and stakeholders provide input, approvals, and business context. This shared operating model ensures that responsibilities are visible, decisions are made with the right context, and teams work with common expectations throughout the project life cycle.

Communication is a deliberate part of the process rather than an afterthought. OctoAcme uses daily standups, weekly delivery syncs, sprint demos, and milestone-based stakeholder updates to keep progress visible and aligned across teams. Risks and blockers are escalated through structured paths, and teams are expected to use a single source of truth for status, decisions, and release information. This keeps work transparent, reduces ambiguity, and helps leaders respond quickly when trade-offs or dependencies need attention.

Quality assurance is embedded throughout delivery, not left until the end. Teams define acceptance criteria and a Definition of Done, run automated tests and linting in CI, and perform security scans and smoke tests before release. For production changes, deployment checklists, rollback plans, and post-release verification are required to reduce operational risk. At the end of each sprint or milestone, retrospectives capture lessons learned and convert them into action items that drive ongoing process improvements.
